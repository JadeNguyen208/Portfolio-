REAL PRODUCT · LIVE ON VERCEL
Case Study 01 · Live Product

Designing for Drowsy Users

A live product, not a concept. SleepSoundMixer shipped on Product Hunt, monetised, built entirely solo.

RoleSolo Designer & Developer
StackVanilla JS · Web Audio API · Canvas
StatusLive · Monetised
ScopeUX · Dev · Launch · Marketing
01 — The Problem

The Problem Worth Solving

Ambient-sound apps assume an alert user. The real one: 1AM, lights off, fighting insomnia — needs play with zero friction.

"Every design decision should minimise the cost of being awake."

02 — Constraints

Constraints as Creative Fuel

No libraries. Six weeks. One-handed, in the dark. Each constraint shaped the build:

ConstraintWhat it produced
No audio libraryCustom Web Audio graph, per-channel gain
No animation libraryHand-tuned rAF loops, particle systems
One-hand mobile firstAll controls in the thumb-reach zone
03 — Audio-Visual Coupling

The Audio-Visual Coupling Problem

Layering Rain + Ocean means two visuals at once. Solved with a blend-state system: each scene gets a dominance weight, alpha-composited by volume.

Volume already signals intent — turn Rain to 80% and its visual takes over.

04 — Monetisation as UX

The Paywall Is a UX Decision

It triggers at session resume after the free hour — never mid-session.

Free-to-paid flow3-tier pricingPayPal integrationSocial proof design
05 — i18n as Systems Thinking

5 Languages, 1 Layout

German runs ~30% longer, Vietnamese diacritics affect line-height, Chinese has no word-break spaces. Fix: fluid containers, no fixed widths, diacritic-safe line-height.

06 — Outcome

What Shipped & What I Learned

40+Ambient sounds shipped
4Session modes — sleep, meditate, focus, AI mix
5Languages supported
What I'd do differently

Underinvested in onboarding — users missed that sounds could mix. Next: a shorter free session and a "save your mix" Pro feature.

Frontend craft in this build
Design System
One token set — accent, type, spacing — across 4 modes, 5 locales.
Responsive
Built one-handed at 375px; controls sit in the thumb-reach zone.
Accessibility
Large tap targets, dark-room contrast, survives 30% text growth in German.
Performance
Hand-tuned requestAnimationFrame loops for low-end mobile.
Tooling Note
Library-free was deliberate — for audio control and performance, not habit. On a team, I use the team's stack.

03 — A Dragon That Breathes

A Dragon Asleep in Its Sanctuary

Not every creature needs to move to feel alive. The illusion rests on one detail: uneven scale — a chest expands more vertically than horizontally as it breathes.

AZURE DRAGON · 5.2S SLEEP-BREATH CYCLE · SHIPPED CSS
Anisotropic Scale
scale(1.015, 1.045) — 3× more vertical, like a real chest.
Anchored Origin
transform-origin: 50% 82% keeps the coils grounded.
Light Follows Volume
Brightness and shadow share the same 5.2s curve.

Breathing vs. pulsing: scale the body unevenly and anchor it to the ground.

04 — One Frame, Many Creatures

Designing a Repeatable Look

One locked style-frame — camera, materials, light — with only the subject varied. 20+ renders, one authored world.

On top, a rarity system adds meaning at a glance:

1–8 Star — common to uncommon King · Saint · Emperor tier Divine — legendary

The creative work wasn't the prompt — it was the system: one frame, one rarity language, 20+ assets that feel authored.

05 — Making the Grid Line Up

Isometric Math That Snaps

True isometric, 2:1 tiles. gridToWorld places a tile's centre; worldToGrid snaps any click to the nearest cell.

REAL APP · LIVE ISOMETRIC GRID

Live in the app — level 13, 40+ plots, every one snapped by that same function pair.

x = (gx − gy) · TILE_W/2  ·  y = (gx + gy) · TILE_H/2

Green = free, red = blocked — placement validates live so nothing floats or overlaps.

06 — Designing the HUD

A UI That Doesn't Fight the World

Three rules: nothing floats bare on the art, one accent colour for every progress state, and an icon-only, collapsible side rail — the farm is the product, not the menu.

The level bar shows 2500/5440 and spells out "2,940 points to level 14" — a ratio plus a goal, not just a stat.

Contrast Strategy
Every HUD chip sits on its own translucent scrim for legibility.
One Accent
Single jade-green accent for progress and active state; everything else stays neutral.
Chrome That Retreats
Icon-only rail collapses to a sliver.

In a visually loud world, interface design is mostly restraint — deciding what not to draw.
